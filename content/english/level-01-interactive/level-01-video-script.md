# Level 1 — Voice-Over Narration Script
**Total narrated duration:** ~35–40 minutes (combined with watch time and interactive pauses)
**Voice style:** Warm, clear, Telugu-accented English. Like a knowledgeable and friendly neighbour. Not a formal news anchor. Not a corporate voice. Think "helpful elder sibling."
**Pace:** Slow and deliberate. Pause after every important sentence.
**Telugu Version:** Translate meaning-first (not word-for-word) — see notes at each section.

## SEGMENT 3 — What Is AI?
**On screen:** Explainer cards

> [NARRATOR — clear, slow, teaching tone]
>
> "Before we see what happens with Ravi's letter, let us understand something important.
>
> What is AI?
>
> In very simple words: AI is a computer program that has read billions of sentences — from books, articles, websites, and conversations — and learned how to answer questions and write text.
>
> That is it.
>
> Imagine a friend who has read every book in every library in the world. Every book on cooking. Every book on writing formal letters. Every book on science, history, business, medicine.
>
> Now imagine you can ask that friend anything. And they reply immediately.
>
> That friend is AI."

**[PAUSE]**

> "How do you talk to AI?
>
> Very simply. You type what you need — in normal words.
>
> Like texting a friend. No commands. No technical words. No coding.
>
> 'Help me write a letter.' 'Explain this to me simply.' 'Translate this to Telugu.'
>
> AI reads your message and replies with helpful text. You read it. You check it. You use what is correct."

**[PAUSE — 3 seconds]**

> "Now, three things that AI is NOT.
>
> First — AI is not a scary robot. Those Hollywood movies with dangerous robots — that is fiction. AI today is a text tool. It does not move. It does not have a body. It reads and writes text.
>
> Second — AI is not magic. It is mathematics and statistics. Very, very clever mathematics — but not magic.
>
> Third — AI is not a decision-maker. You decide. AI only suggests. If AI writes a letter, you check that letter before you send it. You are always in charge."

## SEGMENT 7 — Safety First
**On screen:** Safety warning panels

> [NARRATOR — serious but calm, not scary]
>
> "Now — and this is very important — we need to talk about what AI gets wrong. And how to stay safe.
>
> AI is a helpful tool. But it is not perfect. And some mistakes can cause real problems if you are not careful.
>
> Here is a funny but real example.
>
> Ravi once asked ChatGPT who the current Chief Minister of Telangana is.
>
> AI gave an answer. Confidently. But the answer was the name of the Chief Minister from 2 years ago.
>
> AI's knowledge has a cut-off date. It does not update in real-time. For current news, events, or recent changes — always check a news website."

**[PAUSE]**

> "Four safety rules. Please remember these.
>
> Rule One: Never type your Aadhaar number, bank account details, or passwords into AI. Treat AI like a public space. Do not share anything you would not say out loud in a crowded room.
>
> Rule Two: If AI gives you medical advice, check with a doctor before acting on it. AI can suggest ideas. A doctor can confirm what is right for your body.
>
> Rule Three: For current events, political news, or recent facts — verify on a news site. AI may be out of date.
>
> Rule Four: Always read AI output carefully before using it. It is a first draft. Not a final answer. You are the editor. You are in charge."

## Telugu Voice-Over Notes

- Do NOT translate word-for-word. Translate the meaning and emotion.
- Use common Telugu words where English technical terms are unavoidable (e.g., "AI" stays as "AI" but explain as "ఒక చాలా తెలివైన typing helper").
- "Artificial Intelligence" → "కృత్రిమ మేధస్సు" (first mention only), then just "AI" throughout.
- Keep the Ravi and Priya story details intact — they are relatable for Telugu audiences.
- Adjust local reference: "Hyderabad, L.B. Nagar, Warangal" are perfect. Keep them.
- Tone: Same warmth. Like a trusted neighbour or elder sibling explaining something helpful.

---

## Recording Notes

- Record in a quiet room. No background echo.
- Use a decent USB microphone or a quiet smartphone voice recorder app.
- Leave 1.5–2 second pauses at [PAUSE] markers — these allow visuals to land.
- Do not rush. Speak 20% slower than you think you need to.
- Record one full pass first. Then re-record individual segments that need improvement.
- Separate audio file per segment (10 files) makes future editing easier.
