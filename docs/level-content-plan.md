## Ladder strategy update

Level 1 should not feel like a rushed tutorial. It should feel like a guided first experience where the learner watches, listens, understands, and becomes curious.

- Level 1 is the trust-building level
- Level 2 is the first confidence-building level
- Level 3 is the first depth level (currently free under early offer)

This means the learner journey should be:

1. feel wonder and safety in Level 1
2. try a few real tasks in Level 2
3. go deeper with everyday capability in Level 3 (and beyond)

Note: All levels are offered free during the early access phase.
When the founder decides to activate payments, Level 1 and Level 2 remain permanently free.
Levels 3 and above will revert to their original listed prices at that time.

## What each level should teach

## Level 1: First Step

Audience: first-time AI user

### Objective
Help the learner feel that AI is useful, safe to approach, and relevant to daily life before asking them to do much on their own.

### Recommended duration style
- target 45 to 60 minutes total
- mostly watch, listen, and observe
- only light interaction near the end
- keep cognitive load low and curiosity high

### Content ideas
- story: a family uses AI to plan a birthday event
- story: a student uses AI to understand a tough homework topic
- cartoon: AI is like a helper, not magic
- comic: asking better questions gives better answers
- guided demo: AI writes a polite message and improves it
- guided demo: AI explains something in simple English and then in Telugu
- trust segment: what AI can do well and where it can be wrong
- recap card: 5 safe things to try today

### Suggested structure
1. welcome and emotional hook: 5 minutes
2. what AI means in simple words: 8 minutes
3. story-led everyday examples: 12 minutes
4. guided demos with voice or slides: 12 minutes
5. safety and trust basics: 8 minutes
6. tiny first action by learner: 5 to 10 minutes

### Interaction design
- no heavy typing requirement in the first half
- let the learner mostly watch polished examples first
- use voice-over, subtitles, and strong visuals
- ask for only one or two simple actions near the end
- end with a strong teaser for what they will do themselves in Level 2

### End result
Learner should feel confident enough to try one simple personal task and should actively want to continue to Level 2.

## Level 2: Daily Help

Audience: common household user, school student, parent, beginner office user

### Objective
Turn curiosity into action through easy wins that people can try the same day.

### Recommended duration style
- target 60 to 90 minutes total
- more hands-on than Level 1
- simple repetition across 3 to 5 daily-life tasks
- focus on success, not complexity

### Content ideas
- shopping list planning
- travel packing checklist
- homework explanation in simple words
- Telugu to English and English to Telugu drafting help
- one simple prompt formula: role + task + details + style
- polite WhatsApp or SMS drafting
- festival, family, or school planning help

### Suggested structure
1. fast recap from Level 1: 5 minutes
2. first live task together: 10 minutes
3. three daily-life use cases: 30 to 40 minutes
4. one bilingual use case: 10 to 15 minutes
5. simple prompt pattern and practice: 10 to 15 minutes
6. next-step pitch into Level 3: 5 minutes

### End result
Learner should be able to use AI for 3 to 5 daily tasks safely.

## Level 3: Smart Basics

### Objective
Teach how to get more reliable answers and avoid common mistakes.

### Content ideas
- checking AI output before trusting it
- asking for step-by-step answers
- rewriting text for tone and clarity
- comparing two answers
- saving favorite prompt patterns

### End result
Learner uses AI with more confidence and less blind trust.

## Level 4: Work Smart

### Objective
Teach work, study, and productivity use cases.

### Content ideas
- email drafting
- meeting summary help
- resume improvement
- interview practice
- presentation outline generation
- study timetable creation

### End result
Learner saves time in office or study workflows.

## Level 5: Life Upgrade

### Objective
Expand to family, finance, learning, and personal growth use cases.

### Content ideas
- household budget explanation
- parent-child learning support
- travel planning
- comparing insurance or phone plans in simple format
- habit tracking support

### End result
Learner sees AI as a life assistant, not only a work tool.

## Level 6: Power User

### Objective
Teach structured prompting, workflow chaining, and tool-aware usage.

### Content ideas
- turning one prompt into a repeatable routine
- asking AI for tables, checklists, and summaries together
- using AI with spreadsheets or note tools
- creating reusable personal templates
- identifying tasks that should never be outsourced blindly to AI

### End result
Learner becomes efficient and selective with AI.

## Level 7: Build With AI

### Objective
Introduce simple creation workflows and bridge into project-based learning.

### Content ideas
- create a small website with AI assistance
- build a mini chatbot for a local need
- create a poster, lesson, or business plan using AI
- build a portfolio project from a real-world problem

### End result
Learner can create something tangible with AI support.

- one visual cheat sheet

## English and Telugu strategy

### English
- all master scripts are written in English first
- use simple sentence structure and low-jargon vocabulary
- target around class 6 to class 8 reading difficulty for beginner levels

### Telugu
- translate for meaning, not word-for-word literal structure
- keep interface terms in bilingual form when needed
- use Telugu examples from family, education, travel, and daily routines
- start with Level 1 and Level 2 first, then expand higher levels based on demand
