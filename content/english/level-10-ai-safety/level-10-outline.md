# Level 10: AI Safety & Society

## Who this is for
Every learner who has completed Levels 1–9 and now wants to use AI responsibly —
and anyone who feels worried or confused about the risks of AI in daily life.
This is the capstone level: not about fear, but about being a smart, responsible AI user.

## Lesson objective
Teach learners how to spot AI misuse (deepfakes, misinformation, bias),
protect their own privacy, and become responsible users who help others around them
do the same — starting with their own families and communities.

## Characters

All returning from Levels 1–9, now facing real-world AI safety scenarios.

### Kavya (Warangal, 24)
Gets a WhatsApp video of a politician saying something shocking —
it turns out to be a deepfake. She learns how to check before sharing.

### Srinivas (Hyderabad, 38)
A client asks him to write content using biased AI-generated "research".
He learns to fact-check and refuse unethical requests professionally.

### Padma (Vijayawada, 45)
Receives a scam voice call that sounds exactly like her daughter —
cloned voice AI fraud. She learns how to protect her family.

### Raju (Nizamabad, 27)
Accidentally creates an AI photo of a real person without their consent
for a client poster. He learns about privacy, consent, and image rights.

### Meera (New character — Hyderabad, 19, college student)
Uses AI to write an essay and submits it as her own work.
Faces consequences and learns about academic integrity and honest AI use.

## Story arc (5 acts)

### Act 1 — What Can Go Wrong? (The Real Risks)
A community meeting in a Hyderabad neighbourhood: all five characters share
one AI "bad experience" they had or heard about in the last month.
Lesson: AI can be misused — for fake news, fraud, manipulation, and bias.
Key message: "Knowing the risks makes you stronger, not scared."

### Act 2 — Deepfakes: Don't Believe Everything You See
Kavya's shocking video turns out to be a deepfake of a local politician.
She almost forwarded it to 50 contacts.
Learn: What deepfakes are, how to spot them (face blur, unnatural blinking,
audio sync issues), and how to verify before sharing.
Indian context: Deepfake videos in elections, celebrity fake ads, voice fraud calls.
Skill: Use reverse image search and fact-check websites (AltNews, BOOM).

### Act 3 — Misinformation: AI Can Write Convincing Lies
Srinivas finds an AI-generated "news article" with made-up statistics that
his client wants him to use in marketing content.
Learn: AI hallucinates — it can write confident, convincing, completely false text.
Skill: How to verify AI-generated content before using it.
Tools: Google Fact Check Explorer, snopes.com, Wikipedia cross-check.
Key message: "If AI says something important, check it from a second source."

### Act 4 — Privacy, Bias & Consent
Two stories in one act:
- Padma's voice-clone scam call: how criminals use AI to clone voices from social media.
  Skill: Privacy hygiene — limit voice/face data shared publicly.
  Family safety: Set a secret code word with family for emergency calls.
- Raju's consent mistake: He used a real person's photo with AI to create a poster.
  Skill: Always get consent before using anyone's face or voice with AI.
  Indian law note: IT Act and privacy rights — simple plain-English explanation.
  Key message: "What AI can do and what you should do are different things."

### Act 5 — Becoming a Responsible AI User
Meera learns from her academic integrity mistake.
All five characters come together for a "Responsible AI Pledge".
5 rules every responsible AI user follows:
  1. Verify before you share
  2. Declare when AI helped you create something
  3. Never use AI to harm, deceive, or manipulate others
  4. Protect your own and others' personal data
  5. Teach one person around you what you learned
Lesson: You are now a trained AI user — that comes with responsibility.
Key message: "India needs millions of responsible AI users. You are one of them."

---

## 5 skills taught

1. **Spot a deepfake** — visual/audio clues, verification tools, when to not share
2. **Fact-check AI output** — identify hallucinations, use fact-check tools, cite real sources
3. **Protect personal privacy** — voice/face data hygiene, scam awareness, family safety protocols
4. **Understand AI ethics and consent** — image rights, academic integrity, honest AI use
5. **Be a responsible AI advocate** — teach others, follow the 5-rule pledge, lead by example

## Quiz questions (5 questions, pass 4/5)

1. Kavya receives a shocking video on WhatsApp claiming a politician said something
   controversial. What should she do first?
   - a) Forward it to her contacts immediately so they know
   - b) Check the video for signs of deepfake and verify on a fact-check site before sharing
   - c) Post it on Instagram with her opinion
   - d) Call the politician's office directly
   → Answer: b

2. Srinivas uses AI to write a marketing article and it includes a statistic like
   "85% of Indians use AI daily." He cannot find this anywhere else. What should he do?
   - a) Use it — AI is usually accurate with numbers
   - b) Remove it or replace it with a verified statistic from a reliable source
   - c) Ask AI to confirm the same statistic again
   - d) Add a disclaimer and keep the statistic
   → Answer: b

3. Padma gets a phone call from someone who sounds exactly like her daughter,
   asking to transfer ₹5,000 urgently. What is the safest next step?
   - a) Transfer the money immediately — it's an emergency
   - b) Hang up and call her daughter directly on her known number to verify
   - c) Ask the caller for the bank account number to confirm identity
   - d) Send the money and report to police later
   → Answer: b

4. Raju wants to create an AI-generated poster using a real customer's face for
   the shop's display. What must he do first?
   - a) Go ahead — the photo is from a public Instagram post
   - b) Get clear written or verbal consent from the customer before using their face
   - c) Blur the face slightly so it is not recognisable
   - d) Post it and remove it if the person complains
   → Answer: b

5. Which of these is NOT one of the 5 rules of a responsible AI user?
   - a) Verify before you share
   - b) Declare when AI helped you create something
   - c) Use AI to win arguments and prove you are right
   - d) Protect your own and others' personal data
   → Answer: c
