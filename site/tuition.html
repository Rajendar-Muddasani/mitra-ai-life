How Hindi Will Be Taught

Listen First

Each letter or sound is repeated 5 to 7 times before the child is tested.

Look and Link

Every sound should connect to a large colorful letter, an image, and an anchor word like आ → आम.

Barakhadi Clearly

Matra changes are shown visually and read aloud many times so the learner hears the pattern, not only memorises it.

Daily-Life Hindi

Vocabulary and sentence practice should help learners speak at home, in school, in the market, and while travelling.

Grammar Made Clear

The platform must teach confusing patterns like का / की / के, gender, and verb agreement with many examples.

Write Step by Step

Tracing becomes words, words become sentences, and sentences grow into letters, paragraphs, and essays.

Hindi 0-to-Strong Ladder

H1 · Start From Zero

No-shame start, first sounds, swar listening, and confidence building.

H2 · Letters and Sounds

Swar and vyanjan with repetition, image anchors, and tracing.

H3 · Barakhadi Foundations

How matras change sounds, beginning with clear animated patterns.

H4 · Barakhadi + Numbers

Fuller barakhadi reading practice plus Hindi numbers in real life.

H5 · Vocabulary and Word Reading

Home, school, food, colors, actions, feelings, and smooth word reading.

H6 · Everyday Sentences

Useful Hindi for self-introduction, requests, routines, classroom talk, and market talk.

H7 · Grammar Clarity

Gender, singular/plural, का / की / के, and postpositions taught with contrast examples.

H8 · Reading and Comprehension

Short passages, read-and-retell, question answering, and dictation support.

H9 · Functional Writing

Picture writing, paragraph writing, letter writing, and guided essays.

H10 · Independent Expression

Essay writing, imagination writing, opinion writing, and confident Hindi use.

What Makes Hindi Hard — and Why We Focus There

Barakhadi Fear

Many learners try to memorise it without hearing the pattern. We will teach it with repeated audio and visual transformation.

Grammar Confusion

Especially gender, agreement, and का / की / के. This needs extra practice and very clear examples.

Vocabulary Gaps

Speaking confidence drops when the learner knows the script but not enough everyday words.

Confidence Problem

The tone must never shame the learner. Older children and adults should be able to restart without embarrassment.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this class-wise or skill-wise?

The core Hindi track is skill-wise first. We will start learners at the right level and let them repair weak basics without shame. Class-wise mapping can be added later as guidance.

Why start from H1 and not H0?

H1 already means zero-knowledge entry. That is clearer for learners and parents. "Start From Zero" is the promise, and H1 is the first real step on that path.

Will this replace school?

No. This is practice support, not a school replacement. The goal is to rebuild confidence and strengthen Hindi step by step.

Who is the first launch for?

The child-first launch is for learners who missed Hindi basics and need help with letters, barakhadi, word reading, grammar, and everyday speaking.

Can adults use this later too?

Yes, but the child-first version comes first. A future adult lane can focus more on grammar repair, vocabulary, and speaking confidence.

Do you collect personal data about my child?

No. Child-facing pages should avoid public comments and avoid collecting sensitive child data in the first version.
