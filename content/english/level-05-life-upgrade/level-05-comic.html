1 Skill 1 — Plan your household budget with AI

Lakshmi's story

The month that ran out of money on day 22

Lakshmi manages her family's finances in Visakhapatnam. Every month, money seems to disappear before the 25th. She does not know exactly where it goes. Her husband earns ₹45,000 per month. They have rent, school fees, groceries, and small expenses that add up. Lakshmi feels anxious every time a bill arrives.

👩 Lakshmi · Visakhapatnam

Lakshmi writes expenses in a notebook but never totals them until the money is already gone. She has no system. She feels guilty about small purchases but does not know which category is actually over-spending.

"We earn enough but somehow it never lasts the whole month. I need to understand where the money is going."
💡 The AI approach

Lakshmi tells AI her monthly income, fixed costs, and spending categories. AI creates a full budget breakdown, shows her where she is likely overspending, and gives her a savings target that is actually achievable.

Lakshmi's prompt

Help me plan a monthly household budget. Monthly income: ₹45,000 Fixed expenses every month: - Rent: ₹10,000 - School fees (2 children): ₹4,500 - Loan EMI: ₹6,000 - Electricity, water, gas: ₹2,500 Variable spending categories: groceries, vegetables, milk, eating out, clothing, medicines, transport, mobile recharge, savings. Please create a budget table with suggested amounts for each category. Show total and remaining balance. Suggest how much I can save each month realistically.

✅ The result

AI shows Lakshmi her fixed expenses total ₹23,000, leaving ₹22,000 for variable spending. It suggests specific amounts per category and identifies that eating out is likely eating up ₹4,000 a month — money she did not realise she was spending. She sets a realistic savings target of ₹3,000 per month.

Monthly Budget Plan — ₹45,000 income

Fixed: Rent ₹10,000 + School ₹4,500 + EMI ₹6,000 + Utilities ₹2,500 = ₹23,000

Variable budget (₹22,000): Groceries ₹6,000 · Vegetables/Milk ₹2,500 · Transport ₹2,000 · Medicines ₹1,000 · Mobile ₹800 · Clothing ₹1,500 · Eating out ₹2,200 · Miscellaneous ₹1,000 · Savings ₹3,000

Tip: Reducing eating out from ₹4,000 to ₹2,200 saves ₹1,800 per month — that is ₹21,600 per year.

💰 Budget prompt formula

Give AI your exact income + fixed expenses first, then list your spending categories. Ask for a table with suggested amounts and a savings target. Review it monthly and update when anything changes.

  • 1List your total monthly income (all sources)
  • 2List all fixed costs — rent, EMIs, fees, utilities
  • 3List your spending categories (groceries, travel, eating out, etc.)
  • 4Ask AI for a budget table with amounts and a savings target
  • 5After the first month, tell AI actual spending and ask what to adjust

❌ Without AI

Lakshmi writes expenses in a notebook. Money runs out on day 22. She does not know which category caused it. Cycle repeats every month.

✅ With AI

Lakshmi has a clear budget table. She sees exactly where money goes. She cuts eating out and starts saving ₹3,000 per month — her first consistent savings habit.

2 Skill 2 — Help your child learn with AI explanations

Ramaiah's story

The homework question nobody could answer

Ramaiah is a government clerk in Warangal. His 8th grade son Arjun is struggling with science — especially electricity and circuits. Ramaiah studied commerce in college and does not remember physics. He cannot explain the topic. Tuition costs ₹2,500 per month. Ramaiah wants a better option.

👨 Ramaiah · Warangal

Arjun brings his textbook to his father and points to a chapter on Ohm's Law. Ramaiah stares at the formulas and feels helpless. He tells Arjun to ask his teacher tomorrow. Arjun has an exam in 2 days.

"I want to help my son but I don't understand his textbook. I feel like a bad father when I can't answer his questions."
💡 The AI approach

Ramaiah types the topic into AI and asks it to explain Ohm's Law in simple Telugu-friendly English, using a real-life example. He reads it himself first, then reads it to Arjun together. Arjun understands in 10 minutes.

Ramaiah's prompt

My son is in 8th grade and is struggling to understand Ohm's Law. Please explain it in very simple English — as if you are talking to a 13-year-old. Use a real-life example that a child in India would understand, like a water pipe or a road — not a lab example. Then give 3 practice questions at the end with answers.

✅ The result

AI explains Ohm's Law using a water pipe analogy — voltage is water pressure, resistance is a narrow pipe, current is the flow. Arjun immediately says "Oh! It's like water!" and gets all 3 practice questions right. Ramaiah feels confident helping his son every evening.

Think of electricity like water in a pipe.

Voltage = the pressure pushing the water.
Current = how much water flows per second.
Resistance = how narrow the pipe is — a narrow pipe lets less water through.

Ohm's Law says: Current = Voltage ÷ Resistance (I = V/R)

Real example: If a torch battery gives 3 volts and the bulb has 6 ohms resistance, current = 3 ÷ 6 = 0.5 amperes. More resistance means less brightness!

📚 Parent tutoring tip

Always tell AI your child's class level and ask for a real-life Indian example. Generic explanations are forgettable. An example using water, roads, or local situations makes the concept stick.

  • 1Note the exact topic and your child's class (e.g. "8th grade, Ohm's Law")
  • 2Ask AI to explain it in simple English with a real-life Indian example
  • 3Ask for 3 to 5 practice questions with answers
  • 4Read the explanation yourself first, then read it with your child
  • 5Ask your child to explain it back to you — this is the strongest test of understanding
3 Skill 3 — Plan a complete family trip with AI

Kavitha's story

The trip that never got planned

Kavitha is a teacher in Kadapa. Every year she wants to take her family on a short trip — her husband and two children. But planning feels overwhelming. Which trains to take? Which hotels are safe and cheap? What to see in 3 days? She ends up watching YouTube videos for hours and still not booking anything.

👩‍🏫 Kavitha · Kadapa

Kavitha wants to go to Tirupati or Vijayawada for her summer break. She has 3 days and a budget of ₹8,000 for the whole family. Every time she starts researching she gets overwhelmed and gives up. This year, she still hasn't booked anything even though the trip is 2 weeks away.

"I want to plan a nice trip but there is so much to figure out — I don't know where to even start."
💡 The AI approach

Kavitha tells AI her departure city, destination, travel dates, budget, and family details. AI gives her a complete 3-day itinerary with approximate costs, places to visit, best time for each activity, food suggestions, and travel tips for families with children.

Kavitha's prompt

Plan a 3-day family trip for 4 people (2 adults, 2 children aged 8 and 11). Travelling from Kadapa to Tirupati. Travel dates: 20–22 May 2026. Total budget: ₹8,000 (excluding train tickets already booked). We want to visit the temple, one nature spot, and one fun activity for kids. We prefer vegetarian food. Include: Day-by-day itinerary, estimated cost per activity, budget hotel suggestion, and 3 travel tips for families.

✅ The result

AI gives Kavitha a clear 3-day plan: Day 1 for temple darshan, Day 2 for Chandragiri Fort and a local park, Day 3 for shopping and return journey — with cost estimates that fit within ₹8,000. She books the hotel that evening and feels excited for the first time in months.

Day 1 (20 May): Arrive Tirupati by 8 AM. Darshan at Sri Venkateswara Temple — book online darshan tickets in advance (₹300/adult, free for children). Dinner at Arvind Bhavan (budget-friendly vegetarian). Hotel: Bhimas Residency (~₹1,800/night for family room).

Day 2 (21 May): Chandragiri Fort (entry ₹25/adult) + Talakona Waterfalls — 30 km drive, auto-hire ~₹600 return. Pack lunch from hotel to save ₹400.

Day 3 (22 May): Laddu prasadam shopping, return by 2 PM train. Budget remaining: ₹1,200 buffer for snacks and auto-rides.

✈️ Travel planning tip

Give AI your exact budget and group details. A vague "plan a trip to Tirupati" gives a generic response. Specific details (ages of children, dietary preference, number of days, budget) give you a personalised, usable plan.

  • 1State your travel dates, number of people, and ages
  • 2Give your total budget and any preferences (vegetarian, no overnight travel, etc.)
  • 3Ask for a day-by-day itinerary with estimated costs
  • 4Ask separately for hotel suggestions and food options in your budget
  • 5Verify hotel prices and availability on MakeMyTrip or Google before booking
4 Skill 4 — Compare plans and products using AI

Srinivas's story

The phone plan that wasted ₹600 every month

Srinivas is a 26-year-old bank officer in Rajahmundry. He is on a ₹999 Jio plan but uses only 30 GB of his 1.5 GB/day data. His colleague told him a ₹479 Airtel plan gives faster speeds and better calling. Srinivas does not know how to compare properly. He ends up doing nothing because he is not sure.

👨 Srinivas · Rajahmundry

Srinivas spends 20 minutes looking at both plans on different websites. The comparison is confusing — some show per-day data, some show total. He cannot tell which is actually better value for how he uses his phone.

"I spend ₹999 but barely use the data. But what if I switch and miss something? I need someone to just tell me which one is better for me."
💡 The AI approach

Srinivas lists both plans with their details and his usage habits. AI analyses which plan suits him, calculates the annual savings, and warns him about hidden differences like roaming or SMS limits.

Srinivas's prompt

I need help comparing two mobile plans. Plan A — Jio ₹999/84 days: - 1.5 GB/day data (total 126 GB), unlimited calls, 100 SMS/day Plan B — Airtel ₹479/28 days: - 2 GB/day data (total 56 GB for 28 days), unlimited 5G calls, 100 SMS/day My usage: About 1 GB/day data, unlimited calling, normal SMS. I live in Rajahmundry — both networks have good coverage here. Please compare both plans. Tell me which is better value for my usage, calculate annual cost for each, and list any hidden trade-offs I should know.

✅ The result

AI shows Srinivas that Plan A costs ₹4,282/year and Plan B costs ₹6,243/year. For his 1 GB/day usage, Plan A is better value by ₹1,961 per year. Srinivas keeps Plan A and feels confident he made the right choice — backed by actual numbers, not guesswork.

Annual cost comparison:
Plan A (Jio ₹999/84 days) = ~4.3 cycles/year = ₹4,282/year
Plan B (Airtel ₹479/28 days) = ~13 cycles/year = ₹6,243/year

For your usage (1 GB/day): Plan A gives you 50% more data than you need. Plan B gives you 100% more data than you need. Both cover you — but Plan A saves you ₹1,961 per year.

Recommendation: Stick with Plan A. The only reason to switch to Plan B is if you need 5G speed or if Airtel coverage is noticeably better in your area.

🔍 Comparison shopping tip

Always give AI your actual usage alongside the plan details. "Which plan is better?" gives a generic answer. "I use 1 GB/day and make 30 minutes of calls per day — which of these two plans suits me?" gives a decision you can act on.

  • 1List each option with its exact features and price
  • 2Describe your actual usage honestly (how much data, calls, etc.)
  • 3Ask AI to calculate annual cost for each option
  • 4Ask AI to list hidden trade-offs or conditions you might miss
  • 5Use AI comparison for insurance, appliances, loan offers too — same method works

❌ Without AI

Srinivas spends 20 minutes confused by different websites. He cannot compare properly and does nothing. He overpays by ₹1,961 every year.

✅ With AI

Srinivas gets a clear annual cost comparison in 2 minutes. He makes a confident decision and saves nearly ₹2,000 per year — just from one comparison.

5 Skill 5 — Build a personal habit and health plan with AI

Meera's story

The resolution that lasted three days

Meera is a 32-year-old accountant in Vijayawada. She has been meaning to exercise and eat better for two years. Every January she starts — and stops by the 4th. Her doctor told her she is pre-diabetic. She needs to change her lifestyle but does not know where to begin and what is realistic for a working woman with a busy schedule.

👩 Meera · Vijayawada

Meera wakes up at 7 AM, reaches office by 9, works until 7 PM, cooks dinner, and sleeps by 11. She has about 30 minutes free in the morning and 45 minutes after dinner. She wants to exercise and reduce sugar but every plan she finds online seems designed for someone with 3 free hours a day.

"I know I need to change but every app and YouTube video seems too intense. I just need something I can actually do."
💡 The AI approach

Meera tells AI her actual schedule, health situation, and goal. AI creates a realistic week plan using only her available 30-minute morning windows, suggests simple food swaps (not a full diet), and gives her a 4-week progression so she does not feel overwhelmed.

Meera's prompt

I am a 32-year-old working woman. I was recently told I am pre-diabetic. My goal: reduce blood sugar, lose 5 kg in 3 months, build consistent exercise habit. My schedule: Free 30 minutes every morning before 8 AM. Sometimes 30 minutes after dinner but not always. I cannot afford a gym. I have no equipment — just a flat floor and stairs. I eat South Indian food (rice, idli, dal, vegetables). Please create: 1. A 4-week exercise plan I can do at home in 30 minutes 2. 5 simple food swaps I can make without changing my cooking style 3. A weekly habit tracker table to follow Keep everything realistic for a busy working woman.

✅ The result

AI gives Meera a specific 30-minute morning plan (10 min walk, 15 min bodyweight exercises, 5 min stretching), 5 food swaps that work with South Indian cooking, and a simple weekly tracker. After 3 weeks she has lost 1.2 kg and her morning energy has improved noticeably.

Week 1–2 (Building base): 10 min brisk walk + 5 squats, 5 lunges, 10 wall push-ups + 5 min stretching. No gym needed.

Food swaps: White rice → smaller portion + more dal/vegetables · Fruit juice → whole fruit · Evening biscuits → handful of groundnuts · Curd rice → plain curd (less sugar impact) · Morning tea with sugar → tea with less sugar + one soaked almond.

Habit tracker: Check off each day you complete the morning routine. Target 5 out of 7 days in Week 1. It is okay to miss 2 days — consistency over perfection.

🏃 Habit-building tip

Always tell AI your actual free time — not the ideal time you wish you had. A plan for 30 minutes that you follow beats a 2-hour plan that you abandon. Ask for a 4-week progression, not a 1-day plan.

  • 1Tell AI your exact goal (lose weight, better sleep, exercise habit, etc.)
  • 2Give your actual free time — be honest, not optimistic
  • 3Mention what equipment, space, or resources you have access to
  • 4Ask for a 4-week progression — starting easy and building up
  • 5Ask for small food or lifestyle swaps — big changes rarely stick
